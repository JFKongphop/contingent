01 — the one idea

An outcome token is a binary option

A prediction market on event A — live on Contingent: “ETH ≥ $2,500 at expiry”, resolved by Chainlink ETH/USD; equally a depeg, a hack, a rate decision — has a YES token that pays $1 if A happens, $0 if it doesn’t. It trades at price p — the market-implied probability of A.

That payoff — pay a small premium now, receive a fixed amount only if the event fires — is a digital option. And because the market already discovered p, it has priced your hedge for you. No volatility model. No Black–Scholes. Just p.

02 — how a hedge works (example: a depeg)

Your loss is cancelled in both outcomes

Hedging means holding a payoff that rises when your portfolio falls. You’re an LP with $50,000 exposed to a USDC depeg; the market prices the depeg at p = 4%. You buy N = $50,000 of YES for a premium of p·N = $2,000.

 A = NO · no depegA = YES · depeg
Your portfolio$0−$50,000
Your hedge (YES)−$2,000+$50,000 − $2,000
Net−$2,000−$2,000
Read the Net row: in both worlds you are down only the $2,000 premium. An unpredictable $50k tail risk became a fixed, known cost — an insurance deductible. A perp alone can’t do this — it dies in the crash too. That’s why Contingent pairs them (§03).
03 — protecting a leveraged perp

The perp gives you leverage. The event market caps the crash.

A leveraged long loses fast when ETH falls — and at the liquidation price it loses everything. The live event “ETH ≥ $2,500” gives you the opposite payoff: NO pays when ETH ends below $2,500. Buy NO alongside the long and the crash is partly paid back.

Worked example — the exact trade from our live test: LONG ETH 5x, collateral 100 USDG (size 500), entry $2,720, liquidation ≈ $2,203 (5% maintenance). Protection: NO, cover 100 at p(YES) = 65% → premium (1−p)·N = 35 USDG.

ETH at resolutionPerp aloneNO protectionProtected
Rallies +8% · $2,938+40−35+5
Flat · $2,7200−35−35

Crash, liquidated · $2,150−100+65−35
Read the last row: a wipe-out of −100 becomes −35 — the premium. You pay for it in the good case (+40 → +5). That trade — give up a slice of upside to cap the tail — is what hedging is; the prediction market set the price, so there’s no vol model in sight.

Honest limits. The event resolves once: a crash that liquidates you and then recovers above $2,500 by expiry loses on both legs (−135). The cover is fixed — it pays N, not your exact loss (basis risk). Size N to your liquidation loss and pick a strike near your liquidation price to keep both tight.

04 — one transaction, direction hidden

Open the perp and its protection together — without revealing your side

ProtectedPerp takes three encrypted inputs — collateral, long/short, cover — verifies them once, and opens both legs in a single transaction. The protection side is computed on the ciphertext: isYes = FHE.not(isLong).

Because the side is picked on encrypted data, an observer can’t even tell whether your protection is YES or NO — so the protection doesn’t leak your perp’s direction.

Close both in one click: the perp closes via async decrypt (only the final payout + collateral are revealed, after it ends) and the protection is cancelled with its premium refunded — while the event is still open. After resolution, the protection settles like any hedge. Cover is only granted for premium actually paid: if your free collateral is short, cover is scaled down — never free.

06 — capital efficiency

$2k locks $50k of cover

A prediction market is two-sided and the stakes sum to the payout. You post the premium; the counterparty (pooled LP capital) posts the rest; the winner takes the pot.

hedger   LP
→
$50k pot

Winner takes it. The hedger’s $2k is a probability-weighted margin — that leverage is the whole point of a derivative. Over many hedges, LPs collect the premiums as yield: a fair book at the market’s own odds.

The event leg has no funding rate and no liquidation — a binary event either happens or it doesn’t. The perp leg does (5% maintenance, 0.01%/h borrow fee) — which is exactly the tail the event leg is there to cap.

The honest boundary: your position stays encrypted through the life of the position — when front-running matters. Only a winning payout, a closed perp’s final payout, one liquidation bit, and the aggregate premium are ever decrypted. FHE never flows through a swap; v4 and 1inch only ever touch public or already-decrypted values.
10 — the takeaway

A prediction market turns “will this happen?” into a priced, tradable payoff. You hedge by buying the payoff that pays exactly when your position bleeds — premium p·N, payout N, the market set the price, LPs take the other side. Pair it with a perp and you get leverage with a capped crash, opened in one transaction, and Fhenix keeps size, side and liquidation level private so you’re never front-run or stop-hunted.
